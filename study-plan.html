A PACE YOU CAN REPEAT

Learn a little.
Use it often.

A suggested six-week route through Make a Plan. Aim for roughly two to three hours a week, including a slow game. Stretch the schedule whenever accuracy or available time calls for it.

A 20-minute session

3 minutes: revisit one old miss. 7 minutes: read a lesson and attempt its task. 7 minutes: solve a few unfamiliar positions. 3 minutes: write the cue you want to recognize in a game.

A useful graduation check

Choose twelve mixed positions without looking at theme labels. Explain your answer and the opponent’s relevant resource. Review the misses and repeat them after a delay. Then inspect one slow game for examples of the same skill. Course completion records your study; it does not certify a playing strength.

If the material feels too hard

Shorten the task. Use a physical board, solve untimed, and explain just the next move or structural fact. Return to the relevant earlier lesson when mistakes repeat. Move diagrams physically only after attempting the mental step you are practicing.

Across the full project

The four six-week courses form a 24-week route. You can also use them selectively: board vision supports calculation, planning gives quiet moves a purpose, and endgames teach what earlier exchanges are aiming for.
